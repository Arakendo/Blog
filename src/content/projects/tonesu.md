## What it is

Tonesu is a constructed language built around phonetic transparency, semantic compositionality, generative domains, and reasoning transparency. It includes a normative specification, structured registries, a growing corpus, learning material, and generated review artifacts.

## Why it matters here

Tonesu came before the software projects collected on this site. The work of deciding which meanings deserve primitives, how concepts compose, where scope becomes visible, and how a corpus can challenge a theory gradually changed the approach to software design.

The important transition was not “linguistics inspired some names.” It was learning to treat categories and representations as architectural commitments.

## Questions it explores

- Which meanings are primitive, and which should be composed?
- How can a representation expose scope instead of hiding it?
- When does a category clarify thought, and when does it smuggle in an assumption?
- How should structured truth and prose-heavy explanation coexist?
- How can a corpus provide evidence against a seemingly elegant design?

## Architectural character

Tonesu deliberately separates sources of truth from derived views. Structured vocabulary lives in data designed for validation and generation; prose-heavy concepts remain in Markdown; generated reports make review possible without becoming canonical.

That pattern—owned meaning, explicit derivation, and evidence that can push back—reappears throughout the later projects.
